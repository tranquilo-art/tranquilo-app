About Tranquilo

Why Tranquilo exists

Hey! I'm Mel, the creator of Tranquilo.art, an app that lets you browse public domain art in your browser for free. It's privacy-friendly and ad-free.

The origin of Tranquilo is personal for me. Not too long ago, I stepped back from corporate product & design to take care of my health. While focusing on rest, I noticed that scrolling through the usual apps was more stressful than enjoyable.

As I began to gain energy, I prototyped an app so that I could rest in bed and browse art from The Met. It started with about 40 works of art. As I used it during my downtime, I began discovering fun stories behind the artwork and thinking about how to bring the joy of discovering art to other people. Before I knew it, suggestions from friends and beyond began trickling in. I have been iterating ever since. Now it's grown to over a thousand works across a growing set of institutions, and I am genuinely excited about each new set of works that are brought into our catalog.

A couple of things I'm focused on:

  • An honest product that doesn't extract from users. I'm aiming to make Tranquilo sustainable, not growth-first. I'll continue to support a robust, free, public product. No ads. No cookies. No tracking. You can donate here to help keep Tranquilo free and privacy-friendly.
  • Focusing on public domain (and equivalents). There's so much fabulous open access art out there waiting to be appreciated. I care a great deal about making it easy to find and browse these works.

It's still early stage. I hope you'll give me feedback and join me for the journey.

Openly licensed, always. Every piece on Tranquilo is public domain or otherwise openly licensed (CC0, CC-BY, and equivalents). If you steward a collection under one of those licenses, I'd like to hear from you — see submission eligibility.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tranquilo free?

Yes. Browsing and collecting are free, with your collection saved locally in your browser.

What license types are accepted for submitted collections?

CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-SA, public domain, or an equivalent open license for both the images and their metadata. I don't accept rights-restricted or unlicensed material — see the full eligibility statement on the submission page.

I think I see a bug. How do I report it?

Even though I'm testing, you might find some bugs that escape my visibility. The feedback form is the best way to report one — it notes which page you came from automatically. Please include the device you were using (laptop or phone, and the model) and your browser and version.
